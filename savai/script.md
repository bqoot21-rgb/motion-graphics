# The Savai Karaoke Bar Murders — script (English)

Voice: Kokoro bm_george, speed ~0.9 · ~1,750 words · about 12–13 min

## 1. Hook

On the night of June 13th, 1998, and into the early hours of the 14th, almost all of South Korea was watching one thing. The national team was playing Mexico at the World Cup in France, and in Seoul the match began just after midnight. The streets were quiet, and it was raining. Under one of those quiet streets, in a basement karaoke bar in Gangnam, three people were being killed.

There was a survivor, and there were witnesses. There were fingerprints, footprints, blood and two dozen cigarette butts. The killers had spent hours inside the bar with their faces in plain view. Even so, nobody was ever arrested. Today the case can never go to court.

This is the Savai karaoke bar murder case, often described as one of the most brutal unsolved crimes in Korean history.

So how could three men, seen by so many people, simply disappear?

## 2. The bar, and the night

Sinsa-dong sits in Seoul's Gangnam district, an area of restaurants, bars and late-night business. In 1998 there was a small basement bar there called Savai. It was a danran jujeom, a karaoke bar where customers rent private rooms to drink and sing.

A woman in her 40s owned it and ran it with her older sister.

That night the World Cup kept most people indoors. A taxi driver in his 30s, who knew the owner, was at the bar as well.

Here is the sequence that investigators later put together, mostly from the one survivor's account.

At around 10 p.m. on June 13th, three young men, believed to be in their twenties, came in. The owner's sister was minding the bar at the time. The men took one of the private rooms, ordered a bottle of whisky and fruit, and started singing. They stayed for hours. Reports estimate that they drank around three bottles of strong liquor in all.

Later that night, the owner arrived and took over from her sister. Two women came in a little later. One worked at a nearby restaurant. Her friend, who ran a restaurant of her own, came with her. They had had dinner together and went into another room to sing. The men in the next room were not hiding their faces. Several people saw them clearly.

The taxi driver sat in the main hall with the owner. At about 1:30 in the morning, around half-time in the match, the owner's sister phoned the bar. Everything sounded normal.

So what happened in the minutes after that call?

## 3. How it was done

Not long after the call, everything changed. The door to the women's room burst open. The owner and the taxi driver were shoved inside, already bloodied, with their hands tied behind their backs. The three men followed and kept beating them. Then they tied up the two women as well.

The men went through the bar for money and valuables, then came back. They pulled bracelets and necklaces off the victims and cut open trouser pockets with a knife to search them. Then the beating began. They used fists and feet and stamped down on the victims. According to a former senior police officer who worked the case, they also used a club made from a broken standing coat rack.

According to the survivor's account as reported, when the owner begged for her life, one of the attackers said she talked too much and cut her mouth with a knife. The wound ran 13 centimetres toward her ear.

When it was over, the men went from victim to victim, shaking their heads to check whether they were still alive. The woman who worked at the nearby restaurant had been stabbed in the neck and the side. The blade had missed anything vital. She lay completely still and pretended to be dead, and that is how she survived.

Then the killers set about destroying evidence. They turned on a kitchen tap and let water flood the floors of the rooms, apparently to wash away blood and footprints. They smashed glasses and plates and scattered the pieces across the floor. They wiped what they had touched with newspaper. Then they left.

But one person in that room was still alive. What did she do next?

## 4. Discovery

The survivor got herself up the stairs to the street, badly bleeding and partly undressed. A taxi driver waiting for fares outside saw her and called the police. People nearby covered her with clothing, and she was rushed to Yongdong Severance Hospital.

The sources disagree on the time. Some reports say the call came in shortly after 2 a.m., others say around 2:30 or closer to 3. The finder's name is also given differently from source to source. From the sister's 1:30 call and the report that followed, the killings are placed in the hour or so after 1:30 in the morning.

Gangnam police detectives went down the stairs. People who were there later described the smell of blood as overwhelming. When they turned on the lights, they found three bodies in one room.

The taxi driver had 17 stab and cut wounds. The owner had deep stab wounds to her thigh and back, as well as the wound to her mouth. The woman who ran a restaurant had been beaten so badly about the face that it was discoloured. There was a clear shoe print on her forehead, and her throat had been cut halfway through. Investigators reported blood on the soles of her feet, which suggested she was moved around while the attack was going on.

Some details made no sense. The two women had clumps of hair hacked off with a knife. That would have taken time, when most criminals want to get away as fast as possible. There were also stab wounds to the buttocks, which are not places that cause fatal injury. Detectives were unsettled by these details, and nobody has ever explained them with certainty.

With a witness and this much evidence, why was no one caught?

## 5. The investigation

On paper, this looked like a case that could be solved. There was an eyewitness who had seen the men up close, and others had seen them too. The crime scene team collected four latent fingerprints, 24 cigarette butts and three sneaker prints from the rooms and around the counter. The karaoke machine had even recorded which songs the men sang.

But there was no CCTV inside the bar, and in 1998 security cameras were still rare on Seoul's streets. And according to detailed accounts of the case, only the three sneaker prints were ever formally confirmed by the National Forensic Service as belonging to the attackers. DNA analysis in 1998 was far more limited than it is now.

The money trail went nowhere too. The men took 66,000 won in cash and several bank and credit cards. They never used the cards. The gold necklaces and bracelets never turned up with pawnbrokers or dealers in stolen goods. The men had left no trail anywhere.

So who were they?

## 6. What is known about the killers

Almost nothing. Three men, believed to be in their twenties in 1998. No suspect was ever publicly named.

What follows is analysis and opinion from investigators and experts. It is not proven fact.

The first view is that the killings were not planned. A planned robbery would not usually involve spending hours in a quiet bar, drinking heavily, being seen by other customers and leaving fingerprints and cigarette butts everywhere. The men stayed even after the owner and her sister changed shifts, so more than one person saw their faces. They also left some gold behind. Kwon Il-yong, then head of the crime analysis team at the National Police Agency, argued on SBS's *Unanswered Questions* that the crime was spontaneous rather than planned.

The second view is that, however it started, these were not ordinary men. Professor Yoo Seong-ho of Seoul National University, who carried out the autopsies, said this kind of attack would be hard to carry out unless the person had killed before or had some kind of special training. Dr. Jeong Seong-guk of the Seoul police forensic science unit also said the cruelty and boldness pointed away from ordinary offenders. Some commentators have suggested one of the men may have had special-forces training or a criminal record. That is speculation, and no one has ever been identified.

Again, these are interpretations. No theory has been confirmed.

## 7. Why it can never go to trial

At the time, the statute of limitations for murder in Korea was 15 years. On June 14th, 2013, the deadline for the Savai case passed.

In 2015, Korea abolished the time limit for murder under what became known as the Taewan Law. But the change only covers cases whose limitation period had not yet run out, which means killings from August 1st, 2000 onward. The Savai killings happened two years too early. Even if the three men were identified tomorrow, Korean courts could not prosecute them for these murders.

## 8. Epilogue

So here is what is known: the date, the place, the victims, the number of attackers, what they did, and what they took. Here is what is not known: who they were, why a night of drinking turned into a massacre, why they cut the women's hair, and where they went afterward.

While a country watched a football match in the rain, four people in a basement bar in Sinsa-dong went through a night that was never answered. Three of them did not live to see the morning.

The men responsible may still be alive and out in the world. The law can no longer reach them, but the truth still could. If anyone knows something, it is not too late for the families to learn what happened, even if no court can ever hear it.

This has been the Savai karaoke bar murder case.
